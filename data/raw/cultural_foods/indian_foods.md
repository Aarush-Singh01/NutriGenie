# Indian Foods Nutrition Guide
## Culturally Relevant Nutrition for Indian Diets

### Overview
Indian cuisine is rich in plant-based proteins, spices with health benefits, and diverse regional foods. This guide covers common Indian foods and their nutritional significance.

---

## Staple Grains (Anaj)

### Rice (Chawal) — White and Brown
- White rice: 130 kcal/100g cooked, 2.7g protein, 28g carbs
- Brown rice: 111 kcal/100g cooked, more fiber (1.8g), lower GI
- Basmati rice: medium GI, fragrant long-grain variety
- For diabetes and weight management: prefer brown rice or basmati, control portions (1 katori = 150g)

### Roti / Chapati (Whole Wheat)
- ~120 kcal per medium roti (40g)
- Good fiber and iron source
- Multigrain atta (mixing jowar, bajra, besan) increases protein and micronutrients

### Millets (Bajra, Jowar, Ragi)
- Bajra (pearl millet): high iron and calcium, gluten-free
- Jowar (sorghum): gluten-free, good fiber
- Ragi (finger millet): exceptional calcium (344mg/100g) — great for lactose-intolerant individuals

## Dals and Legumes (Dal, Rajma, Chana)

### Toor Dal (Arhar/Pigeon Pea)
- 116 kcal/100g cooked, 7g protein, 5g fiber
- High folate — important for pregnant women
- Staple dal across India

### Moong Dal (Mung Bean)
- 105 kcal/100g cooked, 7.5g protein
- Easy to digest — suitable for illness recovery
- Used in khichdi, soups, halwa (for children)

### Chana Dal / Chickpeas
- Chickpeas: 164 kcal/100g, 8.9g protein, 7.6g fiber
- Very low GI (~28) — excellent for diabetes
- Used in chana masala, chole, hummus

### Rajma (Kidney Beans)
- 127 kcal/100g cooked, 8.7g protein
- High potassium — good for blood pressure
- Popular in North India (Rajma chawal — nutritionally balanced meal)

### Soy Chunks / Nutrela
- 52g protein per 100g dry — highest plant protein in Indian diet
- Rehydrate before cooking: 18g protein/100g rehydrated, ~120 kcal
- Excellent for vegetarian muscle building

---

## Dairy (Dugdh Utpad)

### Paneer
- 265 kcal/100g, 18g protein, 480mg calcium
- High-quality protein with all essential amino acids
- Low-fat paneer available for weight loss
- Not suitable for vegan or lactose-intolerant individuals

### Curd / Dahi
- 61 kcal/100g, 3.5g protein, good probiotic source
- Easier to digest than milk for many people with lactose sensitivity
- Cooling effect — used in Indian summer meals

### Buttermilk (Chaas)
- Low calorie (~20 kcal/100ml), hydrating
- Probiotic drink common in summer
- Good for digestion

## Vegetables (Sabziyan)

### Palak (Spinach)
- 23 kcal/100g, 2.9g protein, 2.7mg iron, 194mcg folate
- Important for anemia prevention
- Combine with vitamin C foods (lemon, tomato) for better iron absorption

### Methi (Fenugreek Leaves)
- Very low calorie, bitter taste
- May help reduce blood sugar (fenugreek seeds are well-studied)
- High in iron and fiber

### Karela (Bitter Gourd)
- May support blood sugar management (traditional use)
- Very low calorie, high fiber
- Not proven to replace diabetes medication

### Bottle Gourd (Lauki / Dudhi)
- Very low calorie (~17 kcal/100g), high water content
- Good for weight loss and kidney health
- Easily digestible

---

## Spices (Masale) with Health Significance

### Turmeric (Haldi)
- Contains curcumin — anti-inflammatory compound
- Typically used in small cooking quantities (not a supplement dose)
- Best absorbed with black pepper

### Cumin (Jeera)
- Supports digestion, some evidence for blood sugar benefits
- Rich in iron per weight (but used in small quantities)

### Garlic (Lehsun)
- May support heart health (modest blood pressure reduction)
- Antimicrobial properties

### Ginger (Adrak)
- Anti-inflammatory, helps with nausea and digestion

## Protein Guide for Vegetarians

A common challenge for Indian vegetarians is meeting protein targets. Here are practical ways to increase protein intake:

| Food | Protein per serving |
|---|---|
| 100g cooked dal | 7-9g |
| 100g paneer | 18g |
| 100g soy chunks (rehydrated) | 18g |
| 1 cup curd (150g) | 5g |
| 2 rotis | 6g |
| 1 glass milk (250ml) | 8g |
| 1 egg | 6g |
| 30g almonds | 6g |

**Typical vegetarian meal achieving 70-80g protein:**
- Breakfast: besan chilla + milk (18g)
- Lunch: 2 roti + 1 katori dal + paneer sabzi (25g)
- Snack: curd + nuts (8g)
- Dinner: soy chunks curry + roti + dal (25g)
