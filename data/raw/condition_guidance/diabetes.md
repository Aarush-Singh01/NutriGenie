# Nutrition Guidance for Diabetes Management
## General Information (Not Medical Advice)

### Understanding Diabetes and Diet
Type 2 diabetes involves impaired blood glucose regulation. Diet plays a critical role in managing blood glucose levels. The following is general nutritional guidance — always consult a qualified healthcare provider for personalized medical advice.

### Key Dietary Principles for Diabetes

**1. Carbohydrate Management**
- Focus on the quality and quantity of carbohydrates
- Choose low Glycemic Index (GI) foods that cause slower blood sugar rise
- Distribute carbohydrates evenly across meals
- Avoid skipping meals which can cause blood sugar swings

**2. Low GI Foods (Preferred)**
- Whole grains: brown rice (GI ~50), oats (GI ~55), whole wheat (GI ~50)
- Legumes: lentils (GI ~29), chickpeas (GI ~28), kidney beans (GI ~29)
- Most non-starchy vegetables (GI <15)
- Apples, pears, berries (GI 30-50)

**3. High GI Foods (Limit)**
- White rice (GI ~70), white bread (GI ~75), maida-based products
- Sugary beverages, fruit juices
- Processed snacks, sweets, jalebi, halwa

**4. Fiber-Rich Foods**
- Soluble fiber slows glucose absorption
- Target 25-35g fiber per day
- Sources: oats (beta-glucan), legumes, vegetables, whole grains

**5. Protein Sources**
- Protein has minimal impact on blood sugar
- Good options: dal, legumes, low-fat paneer, curd, eggs, lean chicken
- Helps maintain satiety and muscle mass

**6. Healthy Fats**
- Include nuts (limited quantity), seeds, olive oil
- Limit saturated and trans fats

### Recommended Indian Foods for Diabetes
- Breakfast: moong dal chilla, oats porridge, besan chilla
- Dal: all dals are suitable (low GI, high protein + fiber)
- Vegetables: palak, methi, karela (bitter gourd — may help blood sugar), brinjal
- Grains: brown rice, jowar roti, bajra roti, oats
- Fruits: apple, pear, guava (prefer whole fruit over juice)

### Foods to Limit
- White rice in large quantities
- Potatoes, sweet corn (higher GI carbs) in large amounts
- Fruit juices, sugary drinks
- Sweets, mithai, fried foods

### Sample Diabetic-Friendly Meal Pattern
- Early morning: warm water with soaked methi seeds (optional)
- Breakfast: 2 oats/besan chilla + 1 cup green tea (no sugar)
- Mid-morning: 1 small apple or handful of nuts
- Lunch: 2 jowar/wheat roti + 1 katori dal + 1 katori sabzi + 1 katori salad
- Evening: 1 cup unsweetened buttermilk/curd
- Dinner: 1 cup brown rice or 2 rotis + dal + vegetables

*This guidance is approximate. Blood sugar response to foods is individual. Monitor with your healthcare provider.*
