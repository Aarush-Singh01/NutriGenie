# Nutrition Guidance for Anemia (Iron Deficiency)
## General Information (Not Medical Advice)

### Understanding Anemia and Diet
Iron-deficiency anemia is one of the most common nutritional deficiencies worldwide, particularly among women of reproductive age and vegetarians. Diet can help prevent and support recovery from mild deficiency. Always consult a healthcare provider for diagnosis and treatment.

### Key Dietary Principles

**1. Increase Iron-Rich Foods**

*Heme iron (better absorbed, ~15-35% absorption) — animal sources:*
- Chicken, fish, lean red meat (mutton in moderation)
- Eggs (yolk contains iron)

*Non-heme iron (~5% absorption) — plant sources:*
- Masoor dal (3.3mg/100g cooked), spinach (2.7mg/100g), rajma (2.9mg/100g)
- Soy chunks/tofu (very high iron)
- Fortified foods (iron-fortified atta, poha, cereals)

**2. Enhance Iron Absorption**
- Consume vitamin C alongside iron-rich foods
  - Squeeze lemon on dal and sabzi
  - Eat orange or amla alongside iron-rich meal
  - Add tomatoes to curry
- Vitamin C converts non-heme iron (Fe³⁺) to ferrous form (Fe²⁺) — 3-6x better absorption

**3. Reduce Iron Absorption Inhibitors**
- Avoid tea or coffee within 1 hour of iron-rich meals (tannins inhibit absorption)
- Don't take calcium supplements at the same time as iron supplements
- Limit excessive phytates: soak and sprout legumes before cooking

**4. Folate and Vitamin B12 (for anemia prevention)**
- Folate: green leafy vegetables, dal, chickpeas, fortified cereals
- Vitamin B12: milk, curd, eggs, fortified foods (B12 deficiency anemia is common in strict vegetarians/vegans)

### High-Iron Indian Meal Ideas
- Breakfast: poha (fortified) with lemon squeeze + sprouts
- Lunch: palak dal + rice with lemon + methi sabzi
- Evening snack: dates (rich in iron) with orange or amla
- Dinner: rajma or masoor dal + whole wheat roti + tomato salad

### Foods Rich in Folate (Indian sources)
- Palak, methi, coriander leaves
- Moong dal, toor dal, chana dal
- Fortified cereals, whole wheat atta

### Vitamin B12 Sources (critical for vegetarians)
- Dairy: milk (0.5mcg/100ml), curd, paneer, cheese
- Eggs (1.1mcg per egg)
- Fortified plant milks, nutritional yeast
- Strict vegans should consider B12 supplementation (consult doctor)

*Iron-deficiency anemia requires medical evaluation. Dietary changes support prevention and mild cases. Supplements should be taken under medical supervision only.*
