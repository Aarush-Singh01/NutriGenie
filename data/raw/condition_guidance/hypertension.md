# Nutrition Guidance for Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)
## General Information (Not Medical Advice)

### Understanding Hypertension and Diet
Hypertension is high blood pressure. Diet significantly impacts blood pressure. The DASH (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension) diet is well-supported by research. Always consult a healthcare provider for medical management.

### Key Dietary Principles

**1. Reduce Sodium (Salt)**
- Target: less than 2000mg sodium/day (approximately 5g table salt)
- Avoid: pickles, papad, processed foods, chips, namkeen, high-salt snacks
- Use herbs and spices for flavoring instead of salt
- Read food labels for sodium content

**2. DASH Diet Key Components**
- Increase potassium-rich foods (counteracts sodium)
- Increase calcium and magnesium
- Increase dietary fiber
- Reduce saturated fat and red meat

**3. Potassium-Rich Foods (Indian Sources)**
- Banana (358mg/100g), sweet potato (337mg/100g)
- Spinach, tomatoes, rajma, dal
- Coconut water, orange, guava

**4. Calcium and Magnesium Sources**
- Dairy: milk, curd, low-fat paneer
- Green leafy vegetables: palak, methi, amaranth leaves
- Nuts and seeds: almonds, sesame seeds
- Whole grains: oats, bajra

**5. Foods to Limit**
- Pickles (achar), papad, chips, namkeen
- High-salt chutneys and sauces
- Processed meats, sausages (not common in Indian diet but worth noting)
- Excessive red meat (lamb, mutton) — limit to 1-2 times/week

**6. Foods to Include**
- Fresh fruits and vegetables (aim 5+ portions/day)
- Whole grains (oats, brown rice, whole wheat)
- Low-fat dairy (for DASH)
- Legumes (dal, rajma, chana)

### DASH-Aligned Indian Meal Pattern
- Breakfast: daliya (broken wheat porridge) or oats with fruit
- Snack: banana or a handful of unsalted nuts
- Lunch: 2 roti + rajma/chana + vegetable sabzi + salad (no extra salt)
- Evening: low-fat curd or coconut water
- Dinner: dal + brown rice or 2 roti + palak sabzi

### Lifestyle Factors
- Reduce alcohol consumption
- Maintain healthy weight (even 5kg weight loss can significantly reduce BP)
- Regular physical activity (150+ min/week of moderate activity)

*Always follow your doctor's specific medication and dietary advice. This is general preventive nutrition information only.*
